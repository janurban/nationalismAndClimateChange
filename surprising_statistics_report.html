Design

Participants were randomly assigned (groupno = rand(1,2)) to complete two batteries, national pride (6 items, 4-point) and nationalism (4 items, 5-point), either before (group 1) or after (group 2) seven trials. In each trial they estimated Czechia’s rank on a statistic, were shown the true (flattering) rank, and rated how surprising it was (1 = not at all, 5 = very surprising). The design is between-subjects.

Sample

Of 331 started responses, 265 were completed. After the exclusions set in the YAML parameters (completed only: TRUE; Czech citizens only: TRUE; repeat participants removed: TRUE), the analysed sample is N = 263 (before: n = 124; after: n = 139).

Scoring

Pride items (A1 = very proud … A4 = not at all proud) were recoded to 1–4 and nationalism items (A1 = strongly agree … A5 = strongly disagree) to 1–5, so that higher values mean more pride or nationalism. Scale scores are item means.

Estimated rank of Czechia vs. the correct rank (1 = best). Surprise: 1 = not at all, 5 = very surprising.

Respondents placed Czechia lower than its actual rank on every statistic. The share who did so ranged from 57% to 84%. Average surprise across the seven trials was M = 2.51 (SD = 0.83). The most surprising fact was “Overall sustainability among 160+ countries” (M = 3.10) and the least surprising was “Age of Charles University among CEE universities” (M = 1.73).

Rank estimates are right-skewed, so medians are reported alongside means.

Notes

  • The data are the LimeSurvey coded export, downloaded from https://raw.githubusercontent.com/janurban/nationalismAndClimateChange/main/data_coded.xlsx. Correct ranks are taken from the feedback shown to respondents.
  • Internal consistency is modest (see the reliability table), so treat the scale-level effects with caution.
  • Sample exclusions are controlled by the YAML params and can be changed without editing the code, e.g. rmarkdown::render("surprising_statistics_report.Rmd", params = list(drop_repeated = FALSE)).
